Privacy Policy

Effective November 25, 2026

The short version

  • Zuno keeps what it needs to run your account and your chats, and nothing else. No ads, no trackers, no selling your data.
  • Your password is never stored, only a scrambled check of it. Encrypted DMs can't be read by Zuno at all.
  • A few other companies help run Zuno (hosting, storage, email, payments). They only get what their job needs.
  • You can download your data any time, and ask us to delete your account.

1. What Zuno collects

Things you give us

  • Your account: email address, username, display name, and your password (stored only as a one-way scrambled check, never the password itself). Optional: two-factor login, passkeys and your birthday.
  • Your profile: avatar, banner, bio, pronouns, status, profile sound and video, connections you add, and anything else you put on your profile. This is shown to other people.
  • What you send: messages, pictures, videos, voice messages, files, reactions, polls, and the servers, groups and channels you make or join.
  • Reports and appeals you send to the Zuno team, and bug reports and suggestions.
  • Zuno Bloom: if you buy Bloom, Stripe handles the payment. Zuno only learns that you paid and when your plan renews or ends, never your card number.

Things collected as you use Zuno

  • Logins: for each device you're logged in on: when you logged in and were last active, the kind of browser or app, and the network it connected from. Zuno never saves your full IP address: only the first part is kept (203.0.113.47 becomes 203.0.113.x; for IPv6, the first 48 bits), which is shared by many connections and can't point to yours. This powers your Devices list (with a rough city) and new-login alerts.
  • Speed limits: to stop people flooding Zuno, each request is counted for a few minutes by a scrambled tag of its address. The tag is made with a random key that only lives in the server's memory and changes on every restart, so it can't be turned back into an address, and it's never written down.
  • What's read: which messages you've read, so Zuno can show what's new.
  • Activity you choose to share: if the desktop app is on, the game you're playing (from a list of known games) or the song you're listening to. You can turn this off.

Things Zuno doesn't collect

  • No advertising or tracking tools, no analytics companies, and no selling or renting of your data.
  • Encrypted DMs: when a DM is end-to-end encrypted, Zuno only stores scrambled text it has no key for.
  • Calls: voice and video go straight between the people in the call, or through a relay when a network needs it. Zuno doesn't record calls.

2. How it's used

  • To run Zuno: deliver your messages, show your profile, keep you logged in, send notifications you've turned on.
  • To keep Zuno safe: stop spam, abuse and break-ins, review reports, and enforce the Terms of Service.
  • To fix and improve Zuno: look into bug reports and errors.
  • To email you about your account: password resets, email checks and new-login alerts. Zuno doesn't send marketing email.

3. Who else is involved

Zuno uses a few other services. Each only gets what it needs to do its part, and their own privacy policies apply too.

  • Render hosts the Zuno server and its database.
  • Cloudflare stores pictures, videos and files you send (Cloudflare R2), runs the sign-up bot check (Turnstile), and handles Zuno's web address.
  • Resend sends Zuno's emails (password resets, email checks, login alerts).
  • Stripe handles Zuno Bloom payments.
  • A call relay (such as Metered or Cloudflare) passes call audio and video along when two people can't connect directly. It doesn't record them.
  • Anthropic (Claude) double-checks names, bios, statuses and nicknames for slurs and abuse when you save them, and writes a chat summary when you press "Catch me up". Only that text is sent.
  • KLIPY provides GIF search. Your searches go through Zuno's server, so KLIPY doesn't see who you are.
  • Translation (Google Translate, with MyMemory as a backup) gets the text of a message only when you press Translate.
  • ipwho.is gets a login's network (the shortened address above, never the full one) to show its rough city on your Devices page.
  • Services you connect or use: Spotify (if you connect it), and public lookups for music, games, GitHub profiles, Wikipedia and link previews, which only get what's being looked up.
  • Things your browser loads: YouTube (videos in chat and Watch Together), Google Fonts (Zuno's fonts), jsDelivr (emoji pictures) and cdnjs (maps). Like any website, these see your IP address when your browser loads them.
  • Phone notifications travel through your browser or phone maker's push service (like Google or Apple), encrypted so they can't read them.

We share information with others only if the law requires it, to protect someone's safety, or to stop fraud or abuse. If Zuno is ever sold or merged, this policy would keep applying to your data unless you're told otherwise first.

4. Cookies and your device

  • Zuno uses one cookie: the one that keeps you logged in (so private pictures can load). It isn't used for tracking.
  • Your settings, drafts and saved copies for offline use are kept on your own device, and cleared when you log out.
  • For encrypted DMs, each device makes its own key and keeps it on that device.

5. How long things are kept

  • Your messages and files are kept until you or a moderator delete them, or your account is deleted.
  • Devices you haven't used for 60 days are logged out on their own.
  • When your account is deleted, your profile and account details are removed. Messages you sent in servers or group chats may stay as "Deleted user" so other people's conversations still make sense. Some records may be kept longer when the law requires it or to stop abuse (for example, a ban).
  • Backups are overwritten over time.

6. Your choices and rights

  • See and download your data: use Download my data in Settings.
  • Change it: edit your profile and account in Settings, or delete your messages.
  • Delete your account: contact the Zuno team (see Contact). Cancel Zuno Bloom first so you aren't charged again.
  • Turn things off: activity sharing, notifications and connections can be turned off in Settings.
  • Depending on where you live (for example, the UK, the EU or California), you may have more rights, such as asking what we hold about you, asking us to correct or delete it, or objecting to how it's used. Contact us and we'll help. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

7. Keeping it safe

  • Passwords are stored as scrypt hashes. Login tokens are stored only as fingerprints. Everything travels over HTTPS.
  • Files sent in chats are private: only people who can see the message can open them.
  • Two-factor login, passkeys, new-login alerts and end-to-end encrypted DMs are there to protect your account.
  • No system is perfect. If you find a security problem, please tell us: see Reporting a security problem.

8. Ages

You must be at least 13 to use Zuno. If we learn that someone under 13 has an account, we'll delete it. If you're a parent and think your child under 13 is using Zuno, contact us.

9. Where data is stored

Zuno's servers and the services above may be outside your country, including in the United States. By using Zuno, your information is processed there, with the protections described here.

10. Changes

We'll update this policy as Zuno changes. If we make an important change, we'll tell you in your Zuno DM or in the app before it takes effect.

11. Contact

Privacy questions or requests: message the Zuno team in the Zuno Official server, or use Settings → Report a Bug in the app.
